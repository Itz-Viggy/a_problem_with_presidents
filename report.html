U.S. Presidential Lifespans

Vignesh Sundararajan
October 09, 2026

The supplied dataset contains 45 presidents, of whom 39 have recorded death dates. Among those 39, the mean lifespan is 26,097.18 days and the median is 25,673 days. George Bush has the longest recorded lifespan at 34,504 days, and John F. Kennedy has the shortest at 16,978 days. The 6 records without death dates are excluded from lifespan comparisons.

Methods and assumptions

This analysis uses the supplied CSV as a historical snapshot, with no updated death dates. The reference row at the end is excluded. Each president appears once, including Grover Cleveland. Birth year is extracted from the birth date. Lived years and lived months are total completed calendar years and months, respectively. Lived days is the exact difference between death and birth dates, including leap days. Missing death dates leave all three lifespan fields blank in the accompanying presidents.csv. These blanks are excluded from the tables and plot.

Rankings use exact lived days. The weighted average uses each distinct lifespan's frequency as its weight: sum(days × frequency) / sum(frequency). This equals the mean because each president contributes one observation. The population standard deviation divides the sum of squared deviations by 39, describing all recorded completed lifespans in this dataset. There is no mode because every observed lived-days value occurs once.

Conclusions

The recorded lifespans range from 16,978 to 34,504 days, a difference of 17,526 days. The mean and median are close, with a population standard deviation of 4,536.77 days indicating substantial variation. These results describe presidents with death dates in the supplied historical dataset. They do not estimate the eventual lifespans of presidents whose death dates are missing.
